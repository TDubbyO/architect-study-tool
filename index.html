06 — Terms worth having automatic

Glossary

Quick reference for the vocabulary that comes up constantly at this end of the market.

Co-op vs. Condo

Co-op buyers purchase shares in a corporation, not real property; boards can reject buyers for nearly any non-discriminatory reason. Condos convey real property with far lighter approval.

Board package

The financial and personal disclosure package a co-op buyer submits for board approval. Can take 4–8 weeks.

Flip tax

A resale fee set by a co-op's bylaws, usually paid by the seller — not a government tax.

Sponsor unit

An apartment still owned by the original developer rather than an individual owner.

421-a / 485-x

NYC property tax abatement programs for new residential construction.

FAR (Floor Area Ratio)

The ratio of a building's floor area to its lot size — the zoning number that determines how large a building can be.

Air rights

Unused development rights that can be sold to an adjacent lot — how supertalls get extra height.

Pied-à-terre

A secondary, part-time residence — common in condos, often restricted in co-ops.

White-glove building

Full-service building with a staffed, formal lobby and hands-on doorman service.

Prewar vs. postwar

"Prewar" buildings are prized for layout and craftsmanship; "postwar" means the less ornamented buildings of the 1950s–70s.

Starchitect

An architect famous enough that their name alone markets the building.

Billionaires' Row

The stretch of 57th Street lined with supertall towers.

Supertall / megatall

Buildings over 984 ft are "supertall"; over 1,968 ft is "megatall."
